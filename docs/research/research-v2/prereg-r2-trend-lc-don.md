## 2. Hypothesis and null
- H1: IN-EQ instruments whose price is in a channel-breakout uptrend continue to out-earn cash/flat on average (investor under-reaction and herding create serial correlation in returns that persists for months), so a long-only, volatility-scaled, pooled book that holds an instrument only while the rule is on earns a positive net return after costs and tax and beats buy-and-hold. Source: Donchian's channel-breakout rule (Richard Donchian's 1950s-70s trend-following work; the N-bar high entry and shorter-channel exit are public domain technical analysis) as popularised by the Turtle rules (Faith 2007, "Way of the Turtle": System 2 enters on a 55-day high and exits on a 20-day low). The master (s.6.2) fixes the base N = 55 with the exit at N/2.
- Null: after costs, slippage and tax the book is no better than buy-and-hold (B1, B2, B3) and any development winner is selection among N_true trials. Known weakness: a long-only slow rule produces few effective independent trades (INCONCLUSIVE-SAMPLE is likely), it cannot short (the time-series momentum literature earns part of its return on the short side), and the volatility sizing is expressed through the engine's stop-based sizing (section 4), not exact inverse-volatility weights.

## 4. Signal, sizing, execution (detailed enough to re-implement)
- **Signal (daily, closed bars only; one rule, one parameter vector for every instrument):** Trend flag per symbol, initially off. With H_N(t) = the highest HIGH of the N bars BEFORE today (today excluded) and Lo_M(t) = the lowest LOW of the M = floor(N/2) bars before today: if the flag is off and close_t > H_N(t), the flag turns on (entry signal); if the flag is on and close_t < Lo_M(t), it turns off (exit signal). The flag is evaluated only after N prior bars exist. While on and flat (for example after a refused entry) the book may still enter; after a stop-out it waits for the flag to turn off and on again. Volatility estimate `sigma_t` = sample standard deviation (n - 1) of the last V daily simple close-to-close returns, V = the `vol` axis (needs V + 1 closes). No signal is emitted until both the rule and `sigma_t` are available (warm-up).
- **Entries and exits:** a signal on the close of day t is executed at the next bar's open by the engine (a bar with a zero or untrusted open moves the fill to the next usable bar). The book enters an instrument when the rule is on, the book is flat in it; it exits (all) when the rule turns off. An instrument stopped out by the volatility stop is not re-entered until its rule has turned off at least once (so a stop-out is never followed by an immediate re-entry). The strategy marks a position held only on a buy fill, so an entry the risk gate refused is retried while the rule stays on.
- **Sizing (fixed here; a drawdown-only failure is KILL, never a resize):** the long-only engine sizes every order itself from `entry - stop` and the per-trade risk budget, so the rule places a volatility stop at `stop = close_t x (1 - 5 x sigma_t)` (constant `STOP_VOL_MULT` = 5, with the stop distance capped at 50% of price). Position notional is then `risk_pct x equity / (5 x sigma_t x price) x price` = proportional to `1 / sigma_t`: **inverse-volatility sizing, window V**, as the master's "inverse 60-day-vol weights" asks, with the scale set by the risk budget instead of by normalising to gross 1. Risk per trade 0.5% of equity (assumption, master 2.1), notional cap 20% of equity per position, **max open positions 20** (the master's <= 20), no leverage: orders that would need more cash than the account holds are refused, so gross <= 1. The stop is a real stop in the engine (an exit at the stop, 2x slippage on stop exits for equities): it is the price of expressing volatility sizing in this engine and is a declared deviation from a stop-free rule (assumption A1).
- **Kill-switch limit (ASSUMPTION 30%, master 2.1): 30%** drawdown; OFF in the assessment run, ON in the reported operating run.
- **Priority rule among simultaneous signals:** the engine's alphabetical order in the base run; >= 20 random-priority seeds (the harness runs 20; the median is judged, 5th-percentile net CAGR > 0, V1).
- Declared 4c type: **benchmark-relative** against B1 (NIFTYBEES) (the conservative choice: master 2.1 V3 puts a long-only book with invested share >= 50% or beta >= 0.5 on the alpha-t at 3.0, and a pooled trend book is expected to exceed that; `kind` = `benchmark_relative` in the spec); the invested-share rule applies and the absolute t is also reported.
- Pooling declaration: one rule, one parameter vector for every instrument, no per-instrument tuning: yes.

## 10. Author's assumptions and ambiguities (the master is silent; the conservative option was taken)
- **A1 Sizing by volatility stop.** The master says "inverse 60-day-vol weights, gross <= 1". The signals engine accepts only (symbol, entry, stop) and sizes the order from the risk budget, so inverse-volatility sizing is realised through a stop at 5 sigma_t below the close (constant `STOP_VOL_MULT` = 5, chosen before any data so that about 17 positions at a 1.7% daily volatility would use gross ~ 1; it is not tuned and not in the grid). Side effect: a 5-sigma stop can exit a position the rule would have kept; the stop is part of the pre-registered rule. A true weights engine would be the month-end rebalancer (no shift test, monthly decisions), which was rejected because it changes the rule's timing.
- **A2 Crypto selection and dropped pairs.** The monthly top-12 selection gates new entries only; a held pair that falls out of the selection is kept until its own exit signal (no forced sale on universe churn, which would add turnover the master does not specify).
- **A3 Single slippage value.** 10 bps for every crypto symbol (conservative for BTC/ETH); India 5 bps with the engine's ADV model.
- **A4 Whole units.** The engine trades whole units only. To make the rounding immaterial for high-priced crypto units, the crypto spec uses `initial_cash` = 1,000,000,000 (USDT); returns are ratios, taxes and fees are proportional, so scale does not change the economics.
- **A5 `max_hold_days` = 250** (about one trading year; trend positions have no maximum). It only sets the purge gap between train and test; the rule is parameter-free per fold.
- **A6 Account size.** `initial_cash` = 10,000,000 (INR) (the template value was 1,000,000; whole-share rounding at Rs 10,000+ share prices is material at that size). The equity LTCG exemption is an absolute amount, so a larger account is the stricter (less exempt) choice.
- **A7 Donchian bounds.** Channels use bar HIGH for the entry level and bar LOW for the exit level, today's bar excluded; exit length floor(N/2) = 20, 27, 35.
- **A8 Level, not cross.** SMA and time-series-momentum books are long on every day the condition holds (entering at the first tradable day if it already holds at the start of scoring), not only on crossings.
- **A9 Warm-up.** No signal until the rule and V + 1 closes exist; data before the class train start is warm-up and is never scored.
- **Ambiguities for the reviewer.** C1 whether the invested-share clause (V3) should make the type absolute or relative; relative was chosen (stricter). C2 PBO family-versus-book scope (section 6). C3 crypto membership/coverage line is not produced by the harness from the spec. C4 B1/B3 for crypto windows after 2021 need the sealed benchmark engine. C5 the long-only engine cannot take the short side of time-series momentum and Donchian systems that the literature trades; the long-only version is a different (weaker) test than the cited papers.
