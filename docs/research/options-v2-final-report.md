## 1. Executive summary (two minutes)

### 1.1 The verdict
**Nothing passed. All 14 runnable candidates were killed on the development window. None reached a development GO, so none is PROMOTED TO FORWARD PAPER (the only positive outcome this study allows). The default stays a Nifty ETF plus BTC.**

- 14 candidates were run (C1 to C13 and C17, one structure each, 3 parameter levels each). Verdicts: 14 KILL, 0 promoted (script output, section 2).
- No candidate came near. The best net Sharpe on the 2021-11-01..2025-04-30 window among the books that traded enough to measure was 0.02 to 0.03 (C1, C6), against 0.869 for buy-and-hold Nifty ETF. The highest t-statistic of any candidate was 0.49 (C10, a book that was too small to measure), against hurdles of 1.22 to 3.33.
- Ten of the 14 lost money net of costs and tax; the other four were indistinguishable from zero. The clearest losers: the expiry-day iron butterfly C13 (annualised -17.2% on fixed capital, 53% maximum drawdown), the iron butterfly C3 (-12.4%), the put back-ratio C17 (t = -3.29, 1 of 13 quarters positive), the calendar C7 and the delta-hedged straddle C12.

### 1.2 What that means, in one paragraph
Within this test, no options structure that was written down in advance, run on free NSE end-of-day data, after brokerage, slippage, STT, exchange charges, GST and 31.2% business-income tax, earned more than holding the Nifty ETF. That is a strong statement about these 14 structures on this data. It is **not** a proof that no options edge exists anywhere: the test could only have detected a large edge (below), several families were not run or could not be measured, and free end-of-day prices cannot show intraday or quote-level edges. The standing owner decision (hold Nifty ETF + BTC, no new strategy research) is untouched and nothing here argues against it.

### 1.3 What the search could and could not detect
- **Could detect** (calibrated before any strategy ran, section 4): a true net Sharpe of about 3 about 61% to 86% of the time, and of about 2 about 25% to 56% of the time.
- **Could not detect:** a true net Sharpe of about 1. Calibrated chance of detecting it was 3% to 7%. Five years of data cannot see an edge that size; this is a property of data length, not of the gates.
- Those power numbers are **upper bounds**. The calibration left out several later gates (drop-best, robustness neighbours, 2x cost stress, integrity, the all-candidate adverse-regime rule, audit and size gates), so real power is a little lower.
- **Could not say anything about** the monthly covered call, cash-secured put and trend overlay (C9, C10, C11: too small to measure, section 7) and about everything in the not-run list (section 5).

### 1.4 The three biggest caveats
1. **Detectability.** A modest, real edge (net Sharpe around 1) was invisible by design. "Killed" here means "failed the frozen gates", never "proved to have no edge".
2. **Coverage holes.** C9, C10 and C11 were flagged INCONCLUSIVE-SIZE because one lot is too large for the sizing rule: they traded only 10, 10 and 7 of 42 monthly cycles and their volatility was 0.1% to 0.6%. They were also killed on other gates, but the test could not tell us anything about them. (C1 to C4, C13 and C17 also carry a G11 volatility-band flag, but there the KILL stands on many other failed gates.) C14, C15 and the naked-strangle stress cell S1 were never run, C16 was never registered, and no stock option, BankNifty, FinNifty or Midcap Nifty candidate was run (section 5).
3. **The error claim is conditional and the development window is not independent.** The 5% false-promotion claim holds only for a named family of well-behaved null worlds; for a persistent calm/stress regime world it is **not controlled** (family-wise false promotion 4.1% to 20.7%, section 4). Nothing was promoted, so this did not bite, but a future promotion would deserve exactly that suspicion. Separately, the 2021-11..2025-04 window was already the options-v1 holdout window and was consumed as development data (D-006).

Smaller disclosures (section 6): a count-only look at sealed-window trade counts in the first run, a ruined account in C8, a lot-size table error found and fixed, five harness defects, and an accounting quirk that left the sample-size gates of the monthly candidates not evaluated.

### 1.6 Terms in plain words
- **Sharpe ratio:** return per unit of risk (average return divided by its ups and downs, annualised). Buy-and-hold Nifty ETF over the development window scored 0.869 (benchmark B1). Higher is better.
- **HAC t:** a t-statistic is "how many standard errors the average profit is away from zero"; HAC means the standard error is corrected for runs of related results. It must exceed the **hurdle** (1.22 to 3.33 here); a value near 0 is indistinguishable from luck.
- **Gate:** one pre-written pass/fail test. G1 enough independent trades, G2 edge, G3 beats the benchmarks, G4 drawdown, G5 stability across quarters and regimes, G6 neighbouring parameters also positive, G7 result survives 2x costs, G8 integrity and no look-ahead, G9 enough market stress in the window, G10 at least 6% a year on fixed capital, G11 the book is the right size.
- **KILL:** at least one gate failed. A KILL beats every INCONCLUSIVE flag.
- **INCONCLUSIVE:** the test could not decide (TAIL: not enough market stress to judge a short-volatility book; SIZE: book too small or too large to measure; SAMPLE: too many skipped cycles or too few trades; QUALITY: too many stale marks; INCOMPLETE: missing bookkeeping). It means "no answer", not "failed" and not "worked".
- **PROMOTED TO FORWARD PAPER:** cleared every gate on development data; it would then be watched on live paper prices. It is never evidence of profit. Nothing reached it.
- **Forward paper:** simulated trading on live prices from the first session after the tag date; the only clean holdout, because nobody has seen those prices.
- **Benchmarks B1 and B3:** B1 = buy-and-hold Nifty ETF (with a dividend-yield accrual added); B3 = the 50/50 Nifty ETF + BTC blend. A candidate must beat both on Sharpe.
- **Power:** the chance the test says "yes" when a real edge of a given size exists. 5% power means the test is nearly blind.
- **Type A / type B:** type B candidates are structures related to the options-v1 monthly spreads, condors and straddles (or any case of doubt); they were seen before, so their 2012-2021 replication spends no statistical budget and their hurdle is stricter (alpha 0.0139 versus 0.139). Type A candidates are new structures whose rules were written before any 2012-2021 return was computed; their 2012-2021 replication is a second, pre-registered test.
- **Windows:** D1 = 2021-11-01..2025-04-30 (train 2021-11-01..2023-10-31, validation 2023-11-01..2025-04-30). Phase 2 = 2012-01-01..2021-10-31 (replication, development data). S3 = sealed 2025-05-01..2026-10-31 (touched once, only for candidates that reach GO).
- **DSR (deflated Sharpe) and SPA:** corrections for having tried many things; reported, never gating; WEAK means DSR(N=36) below 0.5.

## 3. Data coverage and biases

Sources: `docs/data-inventory-options-v2.md`, `docs/dq-scan-options-v2.md`, `docs/regulatory-timeline-options-v2.md`, `docs/data-inventory-options-v2-benchmarks.md`, `docs/data-inventory-options-v2-yield.md`. All data are free and public; no paid data, no login.

**Option data.** NSE F&O end-of-day bhavcopy, 2012-01-03 to 2026-11-17: 44,721,495 rows over 3,656 sessions (3,068 in the old file format, 588 in the new UDiFF format that starts 2024-07-05). Phase 2 (to 2021-10-31) has 2,404 sessions; Phase 1 from 2021-11-01 has 1,252. Underlyings: NIFTY and BANKNIFTY (from 2012-01-03), FINNIFTY (2021-01-11), MIDCPNIFTY (2022-01-24), NIFTYNXT50 (monthly only, from 2024-04-24), plus 40 stocks chosen by a rule that never looked at returns (top 40 by traded option value 2012-2021; rank 40 = CIPLA). Index closes from 2012-02-21 and India VIX from 2014-05-14 (none before). **Only NIFTY was used by any candidate.** The compact store lives outside git and is not published.

**The two bhavcopy eras.** The old format carries no underlying price and no lot size; the new format does. The two files for 2024-07-05 agree on every field (13,184 contracts, 0 disagreements). A draft conversion wrongly divided volume by the lot; that cross-check caught it.

**Zero-volume rows.** Most option rows never traded: index options 85.7% in 2012, 79.1% in 2021, 63.5% in 2025; stock options 89.0% in 2012 and 51.4% in 2025. A zero-volume row carries a stale close and an exchange-computed settle, not a traded price; entries required signal-day volume and open interest (a liquidity filter), so skipped cycles are part of the results (Table 4).

**Missing sessions and special sessions.** No F&O file exists for the trading days 2013-10-09 and 2021-03-30 (source gap, unrepaired, not forward-filled). Twelve special-session files (Muhurat and weekend sessions) are kept; whether they were tradeable is unverified. Index-close files are absent at source on 11 dates (all in 2014 to 2016) that do have F&O sessions, and `index_date` is month/day swapped inside the files dated 2023-04-06, 04-10 and 04-11, so everything keys on the file date.

**"data_scan_clean" is a judgement, not a scan result.** All 14 candidates were registered with `data_scan_clean = true`. The data-quality scan itself has no "clean" verdict and applied no repair: it lists defects (for example 86,757 traded rows with settle <= 0, 816 with close outside high-low, 5,904 puts priced above strike, 1,278 mixed-lot rows) with repair proposals that were not applied. The judgement that the NIFTY series used is clean enough rests on the engine's own fail-closed rules (unusable contracts are dropped and counted: 3,278 in D1 and 7,920 in Phase 2) and on the orchestrator's reading, recorded in `hardening/STATUS.md` line 144. Read it as a judgement. The scan also ran over the whole store including sealed-window dates (row and defect counts only, no returns); I found no dated addendum listing which sealed-dated statistics it read, so it is disclosed here.

**Lot size history and the lot-table error.** Index lots changed several times (for example NIFTY 50 to 25 in 2024-04, to 75 in 2024-11, to 65 in 2025-10; BANKNIFTY 15, 30, 35, 30), confirmed from 2025-01 by circulars NSE/FAOP/64625 and /70616 (read). Everything before 2025 and every stock lot is derived from value, volume and close (+-2%), not circular-verified. **Error found by the real-data step, before any result:** the date-keyed lot table was wrong for contracts that straddle a lot revision (NIFTY 2025: 499 of 4,482 expiry groups printed a lot different from the table; about 11% of 2025 NIFTY cycles would have been mis-sized). Fix: the lot printed on the contract wins and the table is only a fallback; contracts straddling a revision with no printed lot are marked unusable (commit 956fb56). Files before 2024-07 print no lot, so a few straddling contracts in the old era (for example NIFTY 2015-10-30, 2021-06-24, 2024-04-26) remain table-sized and could be mis-lotted.

**Expiry-day settle semantics.** In the old files the option settle is 0 on expiry day through 2020-01-30 (136,105 traded rows); from 2020-02-06 it equals the underlying's final settlement price, not a premium (530,012 rows). Option expiry value is therefore recomputed from the underlying's final settlement. A real defect was found here before any run: held-to-expiry legs in every pre-2024-07 file would have settled at their last premium mark because the underlying price is blank there (commit 87c3c2a, with a planted-zero test).

**Weekly discontinuities.** NIFTY weeklies exist from 2019-02-14 and are still listed. BANKNIFTY weeklies (from 2016-06-02) ended with the 2024-11-13 expiry, FINNIFTY (from 2021-01-14) with 2024-11-19, MIDCPNIFTY (from 2022-02-01) with 2024-11-18, under the SEBI one-weekly-index rule effective 2024-11-20. Weekly candidates are therefore NIFTY only. The NIFTY weekly expiry moves from Thursday to Tuesday from 2025-09-02 (circular NSE/FAOP/68747, read), which is inside the sealed window.

**Survivorship and selection.** Stock names are fixed by a rule using only 2012-2021 option value, so there is no look-ahead into the development window, but names that became liquid later are absent and some chosen names ended (HDFC 2023-07-12, TATAMOTORS 2025-10-23, ZEEL 2024-06-27; reasons unverified). ## 7. Interpretation: why these structures lose net of costs on free end-of-day data

This section states only what the verdict files support. Each structure was tested at one pre-written base setting with two neighbours, on one window, so none of it is a statement about the structure in general.

- **Near zero or below zero after costs.** The net annualised mean on fixed capital is negative for 10 of 14 candidates; the largest is +0.28% a year (C6), against a 6% floor. Doubling slippage and brokerage makes every candidate that traded enough worse, and the extra drag from doubling is about 1.4% a year for C1, 3.5% for C2, 7.1% for C3 and 13.3% for C13 (Appendix Table 5; derived by assuming 52 weekly and 12 monthly periods a year). So even the best-behaved defined-risk credit structures (C1, C4, C5) earn net returns indistinguishable from zero while paying about that much for slippage and brokerage alone. I found no gross-of-cost series in the committed files, so "near zero before costs" is not claimed.
- **Debit structures bleed.** Long-volatility books (C6 long straddle: t 0.05; C12 delta-hedged long straddle: t -1.96, Sharpe -1.06, 2 of 13 quarters positive) and the calendar C7 (t -1.28, 3 of 13 quarters) pay premium and slippage in most cycles and need a large move to recover it. The ruin of the C8 cell is the same mechanism at its extreme.
- **Short structures carry the tail.** The iron butterfly C3 (maximum drawdown 0.425 against a 0.25 limit), the expiry-day butterfly C13 (0.534 and a ruined neighbour cell) and the put back-ratio C17 (1 of 13 quarters positive) lose heavily, and C2 failed its drawdown-to-volatility leg. The development window holds a real stress sample, which is why G9 was met and these books could be judged.
- **Too small to measure, which is not the same as failing.** The monthly covered call C9, cash-secured put C10 and trend overlay C11 were flagged INCONCLUSIVE-SIZE (G11): with a Rs 1 crore account and the sizing rule, one lot of a monthly future or short put does not fit the risk budget in most months, so only 10, 10 and 7 monthly cycles traded in D1 and the books' volatility was 0.6%, 0.1% and 0.3%. They were also killed (G2 and others), but those verdicts rest on a handful of trades next to a near-flat equity curve. **The test could not say anything about them.** A larger account or smaller lots would be a different experiment and was not run.
- **What the test could not detect:** a true net Sharpe of about 1 (3% to 7% power); an edge living only in thinly traded contracts, which the liquidity filter skips (Phase 2 skip shares reached 73% to 79% for C7, C8 and C17, so Phase 2 was not a usable replication for them); edges needing intraday timing or quotes; edges in stocks, BankNifty, FinNifty or Midcap Nifty; parameter levels outside the three registered per candidate (only the middle level was a hypothesis, no argmax); lumpy edges in a few stress weeks; and anything in the sealed window, which was never opened.
